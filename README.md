# Digital-Initiatives-School-Performance-India
An empirical study examining the impact of digital infrastructure on school performance in India. Uses UDISE data from Goa, Delhi, and Maharashtra, applying statistical analysis and multiple linear regression in R to evaluate the relationship between technology and academic outcomes.



## Overview
This research examines whether the availability of digital infrastructure in schools contributes to improved academic performance in India. The study investigates the relationship between digital facilities and Class 10 and Class 12 examination pass percentages across Goa, Delhi, and Maharashtra.

## Research Objectives
- Examine the relationship between digital infrastructure and student academic performance.
- Analyse whether access to digital facilities translates into higher examination pass rates.
- Understand disparities in the distribution of digital resources across schools.
- Evaluate the effectiveness of technology-based educational initiatives.

## Dataset
The study uses cross-sectional data collected from the Unified District Information System for Education (UDISE) reports.

**Geographical Coverage:** Goa, Delhi, and Maharashtra.

**Sample Size:** 75 observations.

### Variables

**Dependent Variable:**
- Passing Percentage – Percentage of students passing Class 10 and Class 12 examinations.

**Independent Variables:**
- Desktop Computers
- Laptops
- Tablets
- Projectors
- Digital Boards (DigiBoards)

## Methodology
The research employs quantitative analysis using Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) and Multiple Linear Regression to evaluate the relationship between digital facilities and examination performance.

The statistical techniques include:
- Descriptive Statistics
- Correlation Analysis
- Multiple Linear Regression
- Variance Inflation Factor (VIF)
- Breusch-Pagan Test
- Goldfeld-Quandt Test
- Residual Diagnostic Analysis

## Tools & Technologies
- **R:** Statistical computing and data analysis.
- **RStudio:** Regression modelling and statistical diagnostics.
- **Microsoft Excel:** Data organisation and preparation.

## Key Findings
- Digital facilities show weak correlations with student passing percentages.
- None of the five digital infrastructure variables has a statistically significant impact on passing percentages in the final regression model.
- The final regression model explains approximately 2.82% of the variation in passing percentages.
- Significant disparities exist in the distribution of digital resources across schools.
- Effective technology integration, teacher training, and infrastructure development may be more important than simply increasing the number of digital devices.

## Conclusion
The research finds that the availability of digital facilities alone does not necessarily translate into improved academic performance. A comprehensive approach involving effective classroom integration, teacher training, quality digital content, and equitable infrastructure is essential for improving the effectiveness of digital education in India.
